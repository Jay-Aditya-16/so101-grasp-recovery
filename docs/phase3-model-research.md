# Grasp recovery and world-model research: implementation review

## Decision and present capability

Prioritize understanding the current placement feasibility failure and then building a **grasp-specific**, outcome-conditioned experiment in MuJoCo. The existing Phase III learner ranks six box placement targets after a scripted pick; its binary outcome is a placement proxy. It neither explores grasps nor models changing object pose. The available matched test on seed 1030 shows a lift and a failure to find an accepted transfer pose in both versions. This is a single case, not an estimate of a general rate. A world model or a bandit cannot select a feasible candidate if the current candidate generator produces none; nor can it safely overturn physical checks.

## BORGES: direct relevance and missing prerequisites

The [BORGES paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v155/danielczuk21a.html) studies online grasp exploration for a polyhedral object. It assumes the robot can distinguish object stable poses, generate and execute candidate grasps for each pose, and determine grasp success. Its theoretical reduction to per-pose bandits uses assumptions about the relationship between toppling and landing transitions. The paper evaluates repeated sequential grasp, release, and toppling interactions; do not infer reliable one-shot learning from those results. In its published experiments, BORGES uses UCB or Thompson sampling over grasp choices conditional on pose. The results do not establish a benefit for our arm, camera or simulator.

**A bounded experiment inspired by BORGES**, not a reproduction, would implement:

1. Identify an object/pose class from images. The current red centroid is not a stable-pose estimator. Use known cube geometry in simulation as an explicitly restricted initial case, and abstain if the view cannot distinguish a proposed class. Keep simulator pose ground truth for evaluation only. Rotational symmetry makes visually identical cube orientations indistinguishable; do not invent orientation labels.
2. Generate a small, named set of distinct grasp candidates in the *estimated object frame*: an approach pose, closure target and retreat/lift plan per candidate. Each must respect the measured gripper opening and modelled arm limits and have an IK/trajectory validation result. Log every candidate and its reason for rejection. A list of arbitrary XYZ nudges is not a grasp model: candidate features must correspond to observed object geometry, reachable contact surfaces, estimated gripper alignment and approach constraints. Candidate geometry must not use the simulator's hidden object state.
3. Log `(episode, timestamp, object/pose class, observed two-view context, candidate ID and geometry, feasibility checks, chosen action, outcome, termination reason)`. Distinguish failed lift from no candidate, aborted, obscured or interrupted; the last cases are censored, not automatic reward zero. Measure elapsed control time and the effect of active sensing before introducing a retry.
4. As a transparent simulation baseline, for each sufficiently identified `(pose class, candidate)` maintain `Beta(alpha, beta)` with initial `(1,1)` and, *only* after verified success/failure, `alpha += r`, `beta += 1-r`. The posterior mean is `alpha/(alpha+beta)`: one failure changes a fresh candidate's mean from `1/2` to `1/3`. Its posterior expresses a conditional stationary Bernoulli model, not calibrated safety or an assurance of success. Thompson sampling draws a score from each candidate's posterior; a failure does **not** guarantee that an action can never be sampled again. Restrict any exploration to candidates that pass separately validated feasibility checks, and start with shadow scoring; do not permit this code to actuate hardware.
5. Score actual grasp outcomes independently of placement outcomes: a simulator-only held-out label may require bilateral contact plus sustained elevation with no object/table support and low relative gripper-object motion. Contact or gripper opening alone is insufficient. Validate any deployable sensor-based verifier separately against this label; an overhead-only camera can lose the target, so unknown must remain unknown. Ground-truth simulation contacts and poses may label evaluations, never become controller observations.
6. A proper ablation compares scripted grasp, feasible candidates with fixed order, feasible uniformly randomized candidate and learned per-pose selection. Equalize retries, scene, time budget, observation, planner and scoring. Separate online-learning and frozen-policy held-out trials; retain attempts that fail before candidate selection. Randomize pair order and predeclare seeds and horizons. Report both first-attempt and eventual grasp/placement successes, censorship, IK rejections, collisions, no-candidate rate, time and uncertainty. Compare learning curves across multiple training seeds, not one cherry-picked trajectory.

The one-cube case will initially have only one visually distinguishable stable pose class, so it tests correct outcome bookkeeping and choice between grasp candidates, **not** generalization to multiple stable poses. A failed grasp can change scene/object state; reobserve before another attempt. The paper's post-success dropping and toppling transitions are not present in our setup, so its regret guarantees cannot be imported.

## World models and JEPA: a separate prediction experiment

[V-JEPA 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985) learns video representations; the separate V-JEPA 2-AC model was adapted using robot interaction data to predict action-conditioned latent futures and support planning. The [official repository](https://github.com/facebookresearch/vjepa2) lists the released AC model as ViT-g with a roughly 1B-parameter encoder, whereas its 80M-parameter V-JEPA 2.1 ViT-B is a visual encoder, not the published AC planner. The repo recommends CUDA and documents a `decord` macOS compatibility issue. Neither memory usage on the available L4 nor 20 Hz M1 latency has been measured in this project. The published robot and action representation are different from ours: feeding six SO-101 absolute joint targets to a model trained for another action space without a verified mapping is invalid.

[LeWorldModel](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19312) reports a roughly 15M-parameter joint-embedding model; its [open implementation](https://github.com/lucas-maes/le-wm) is a candidate for an isolated L4 experiment, not evidence that its training fits our L4 runtime or that it works on SO-101. [DINO-WM](https://dino-wm.github.io/) is another action-conditioned latent-planning reference with [released code](https://github.com/gaoyuezhou/dino_wm); its installation instructions target a different MuJoCo/Linux environment, so direct compatibility is unverified. None is presently installed, trained, tested or connected to this controller.

A testable first model predicts observed **future** visual features given historical observations and recorded actions. Abstractly, `z_t = E(o_t)` and `z_hat_(t+h) = F(z_(t-k:t), a_(t:t+h-1))`; evaluate prediction error against held-out `E(o_(t+h))` alongside persistence `z_hat_(t+h)=z_t` and an action-shuffled model. A low latent error by itself need not preserve contacts, object identity or physical accuracy, and can be dominated by the static background. Monitor feature variance/collapse and task-relevant held-out metrics, and check whether replacing the actual actions with shuffled actions changes predictions. The notation is a proposed test, not a claim to reproduce any paper's full loss.

Data must contain time-aligned top and wrist RGB, joint states, commanded action units/order, frame rate, calibration/version and termination reason; both actual observation and predicted outcome must be time-consistent. Split by **episode and initial condition** before constructing windows, avoiding overlapping clips across train/test. Collect enough diverse successes and failures before claiming a model learns recovery. Measure number of trajectories, GPU peak memory, training and inference throughput, and M1 peak memory/latency rather than assuming compatibility. Initially run the model in offline or shadow mode; compare against the original controller on predeclared held-out cases before any influence on action. An apparent future image or low model error is not a substitute for IK, collision or contact checks.

## Implementation gates and priority

| Priority | Work | Required evidence before increasing scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Diagnose transfer feasibility from logged IK candidates and joint configurations; separate pose, solver and collision rejections. | Reproducible ablations with unchanged constraints and candidate provenance; no weakening limits solely to make the seed pass. |
| 2 | Implement object-frame grasp candidates, observability checks and independent grasp-success labels in simulation. | Coordinate-frame tests, invalid/occluded input abstention, candidate feasibility tests, known outcomes and episode-level logging. |
| 3 | Add a BORGES-inspired pose/candidate bandit in shadow, then controlled simulator trials. | Separate train/test seeds; fixed/uniform/no-memory baselines and confidence intervals across multiple trials. |
| 4 | Build an action-conditioned JEPA-style prediction benchmark on recorded SO-101 trajectories. | It beats persistence and action shuffling on held-out task-relevant outcomes and fits measured L4 and M1 budgets. |
| 5 | Only then test model-informed ranking or grasp replanning in MuJoCo. | Paired closed-loop improvements with no worse measured failures, bounded execution time and the same feasibility checks. |

All current work is a simulation research prototype. Discrete collision samples and the current exclusion for expected cube contact do not certify swept carried-object geometry, contact force, torque, speed, or real-world safety. Hardware deployment requires independent calibration and validation.
