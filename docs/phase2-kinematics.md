# Phase 2 kinematics and observation recovery

The simulation-only approach planner takes an image-derived position, asks the tutorial's damped least-squares inverse-kinematics solver for a top-down 8 cm approach, and independently recomputes forward kinematics. It checks joint ranges, position error <= 5 mm, tool direction dot product >= 0.98, and 101 discrete interpolated joint positions for MuJoCo contacts penetrating more than 1 mm. It produces a candidate, never commands the robot. These thresholds and the interpolation are exploratory; they are not continuous collision detection, dynamic validation, safety guarantees, or hardware limits. Finger opening, grasp closure, object transport and placement are not planned.

For four starting frames all four candidates had valid joint bounds, FK target residuals below 1 mm and no sampled penetration. At their alert frames all four abstained because the object was not reliably visible. In the successful grasp case, a sampled direct return to home began with 1.84 mm of cube/jaw penetration, illustrating why an unknown alert must not trigger a blind return. Results are in `reports/phase2-kinematics.json`.

For eight recorded joint states, MuJoCo's site-position Jacobian differed from central finite differences by at most 8.87e-11 m/rad (`reports/phase2-jacobian-check.json`). This verifies a numerical derivative at those simulator states only; it does not validate real-robot calibration, dynamics, contact models, IK convergence, or general safety.

With wrist-camera extrinsics updated from the corresponding recorded joint state, the Phase 1 alert frames yielded one estimate out of four, with 6.32 mm error against simulator ground truth (`reports/phase2-wrist-alert.json`). A fixed plane and color centroid do not provide calibrated wrist-view accuracy.

`scripts/live_reobserve.py` restores a captured suspected-miss state in MuJoCo, checks a sampled joint interpolation to the home pose, shows the return in the live viewer, then detects the cube again. Two isolated simulator experiments, seeds 1001 and 1002, recovered a view with errors of 2.06 mm and 1.94 mm; simulator-reported cube displacement was near zero in those cases. These are replayed failure states, not full ACT-and-recovery episodes. The return remains experimental: no continuous collision checking, dynamics prediction, moving-object proof, validation on held-out trials, grasp attempt, placement, or physical-arm safety validation exists. The sampled path accepts up to 1 mm penetration and must not be treated as a clearance guarantee. See `reports/phase2-reobserve-seed1001.json` and `reports/phase2-reobserve-seed1002.json`.

The next work is to connect failure detection, guarded reobservation, observation-based grasp generation, bounded approach/closure/lift and verified placement in one live rollout. Compare ACT alone, a retry baseline, and the integrated recovery on matched held-out seeds, including false alarms and failed approaches. The current results cannot establish improvement in grasp success.
